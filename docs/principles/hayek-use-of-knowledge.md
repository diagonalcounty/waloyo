# Hayek — *The Use of Knowledge in Society* (1945)

Source: F. A. Hayek, “The Use of Knowledge in Society,” *American Economic Review* 35 (September 1945): 519–530.  
Research issue: [#20](https://github.com/diagonalcounty/waloyo/issues/20).

Hayek’s claim in this essay: the economic problem is **not** allocating a given pile of stuff. It is using knowledge that **never exists in one head**. Wealth grows when people who know local facts can act, and when **prices** carry the rest of the news. A planner who demands all the facts first cannot keep up.

These cards are from that essay only. Later Hayek (*Road to Serfdom*, *Constitution of Liberty*) stays on #20 as a follow-up pass.

---

## Card: `hayek-dispersed-knowledge`

**Name:** No one has the whole picture.

**Source:** Hayek 1945, opening argument — knowledge exists only as “dispersed bits of incomplete and frequently contradictory knowledge” held by separate people.

**Mechanism:** Useful facts are scattered. A “rational plan” written from the center is written in ignorance. Wealth grows when many incomplete knowers act, not when one pretended knower decides.

**Player does:** Act on what *this couple* can see (a field, a neighbor, a shortage here) without waiting for a complete survey.

**Player fails:** Freeze until a ledger or official has “all the facts.” The season moves; the surplus does not.

**Chapters:** open (almost every era)

---

## Card: `hayek-man-on-the-spot`

**Name:** The person on the spot knows something the capital does not.

**Source:** Hayek 1945 — “knowledge of particular circumstances of time and place”; almost everyone has “unique information of which beneficial use might be made” only if the decision is left to them.

**Mechanism:** The fleeting fact (the cart broke, the well is low, this buyer will pay today) never makes it into statistics in time. Leaving the decision with the person who *has* the fact turns that fact into goods.

**Player does:** Choose locally — plant, sell, store, hire — from what they just learned, even if it disagrees with last year’s plan or a distant instruction.

**Player fails:** Send every choice up a chain. The fact goes stale; the opportunity is gone.

**Chapters:** open (strong wherever there is a distant ruler, board, or plan)

---

## Card: `hayek-prices-as-signals`

**Name:** Prices are news, compressed.

**Source:** Hayek 1945 — the price system as a “mechanism for communicating information”; tin-mine example: most people need only know that tin is dearer, not *why*.

**Mechanism:** A price change is a telegram. It tells strangers to conserve, substitute, or produce more **without** sharing the whole story. That coordination is how a society uses knowledge no one person has.

**Player does:** Treat a rising or falling price as a reason to change what they make, buy, or save — and let others do the same.

**Player fails:** Ignore the price, or freeze it, and keep producing as before. Local knowledge never reaches anyone else.

**Chapters:** open (needs some form of exchange; weakest in a pure gift camp, strongest once markets exist)

---

## Card: `hayek-adaptation-not-allocation`

**Name:** The problem is change, not a fixed pie.

**Source:** Hayek 1945 — if things never changed, a once-and-for-all plan might work; the real problem is “rapid adaptation to changes in the particular circumstances of time and place.”

**Mechanism:** Wealth is not a stock to divide. It is a stream that continues only if people keep adjusting. A game that treats resources as a given pile to assign (Civ-style “allocate production”) is solving the wrong problem.

**Player does:** When something shifts (weather, a road, a neighbor’s surplus), change the plan this turn. Reward is surviving the *next* change, not maxing a static yield.

**Player fails:** Lock last season’s recipe. The world moved; the household did not.

**Chapters:** open (every chapter that has weather, trade, or other people)

---

## Card: `hayek-planning-as-trap`

**Name:** A plan that requires everyone’s knowledge cannot run.

**Source:** Hayek 1945 — statistics omit the particular; tacit and local knowledge “cannot enter into statistics and therefore cannot be conveyed to any central authority in statistical form.”

**Mechanism:** Central direction can only see what fits a form. What does not fit a form is exactly the knowledge that would have created the next surplus. This is why command systems fail even when the commander is smart and kind.

**Player does:** Prefer rules and prices that let many people adjust over a single schedule the couple (or a ruler) writes for everyone.

**Player fails:** Win by filling the official plan. The plan is complete; the larder is not.

**Chapters:** open (pairs with [#22](https://github.com/diagonalcounty/waloyo/issues/22) as the failure chapter or the tempting shortcut)

## What this already tells us about chapters

We still should not pick chapter one. We *can* see the shape of play:

- A chapter needs **local facts the player can see** and **a signal (price or equivalent)** that carries news they cannot see.
- A chapter where the “smart” move is to report everything upward and wait will teach the *failure* of these cards.
- A chapter with no exchange at all can teach man-on-the-spot, but not prices-as-signals. First chapter, if we want the full Hayek loop, needs some kind of trade.
